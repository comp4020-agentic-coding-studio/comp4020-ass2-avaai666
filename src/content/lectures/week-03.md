On 7 January 1954 a joint Georgetown and IBM demonstration translated a few
dozen Russian sentences into English with a vocabulary of around 250 words and
six grammar rules. It was reported as a solved problem within five years. In
1966 the ALPAC report concluded otherwise and the funding stopped.

That is the documented part. The part everyone actually repeats is that some
early system rendered *the spirit is willing but the flesh is weak* as *the
vodka is good but the meat is rotten*, and no version of that story names a
system, a date, or a witness.

- what a six-rule system can and cannot get wrong
- ALPAC, and what "quality" meant to the people who measured it
- the vodka story, and the shape of an unsourceable claim
