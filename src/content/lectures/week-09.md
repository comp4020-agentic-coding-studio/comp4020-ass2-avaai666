Nothing in this lecture is funny, and the register of this course changes here on
purpose.

In 1980 a young man arrived unconscious at a Florida hospital. His family used
the Spanish word <span lang="es">intoxicado</span>, which in that usage means
poisoned or made ill by something ingested. It was taken as *intoxicated*. He
was treated for a drug overdose. He had suffered a brain haemorrhage, and he left
the hospital quadriplegic.

In 1840 the English and Māori texts of the Treaty of Waitangi did not say the
same thing. The English text ceded sovereignty. The Māori text ceded
<span lang="mi">kāwanatanga</span>, and retained
<span lang="mi">rangatiratanga</span>. The disagreement is not settled and is not
a curiosity.
