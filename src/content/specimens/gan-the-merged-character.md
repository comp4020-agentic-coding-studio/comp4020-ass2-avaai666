The dish is <span lang="zh-Hans">干爆鸭子</span>: duck, dry-fried, hot.

<blockquote data-specimen>
  <p>Fuck the duck until exploded</p>
</blockquote>

## The merger

Simplification collapsed <span lang="zh-Hans">乾</span> (*gān*, dry) and
<span lang="zh-Hans">幹</span> (*gàn*, to do — and, in some registers, to do to
somebody) into the single form <span lang="zh-Hans">干</span>. Three senses now
share one key, and the reading that distinguishes them is tonal and contextual,
neither of which survives into a lookup table.

<span lang="zh-Hans">爆</span> is the cooking method. Rendered as *explode*, it
is not even wrong: that is what the character says outside a kitchen.

## What the output tells you about the table

It ranked the vulgar sense first. That is not a bug in the merger; the merger
only made the senses share a key. Something downstream decided which one was
most likely, and it decided using a corpus in which people write about doing
things to each other far more often than they write about dry-frying poultry.

The output is therefore a readable statement about a text collection nobody
published.

## What it does not tell you

Whether a human was in the loop, when the sign was made, or whether the menu
existed at all outside the photograph. The corpus holds it at `secondary` for
that last reason and no other.
