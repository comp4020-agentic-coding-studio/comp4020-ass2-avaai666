The story is that the Chevrolet Nova sold badly in Spanish-speaking markets
because buyers read the name as <span lang="es">no va</span> — *it does not go*.

## Why it is apocryphal

The car sold adequately in the markets the story concerns.
<span lang="es">Nova</span> and <span lang="es">no va</span> are not homophones
in ordinary speech; the stress falls differently and the juncture is audible.
And the reading the anecdote requires — a brand name parsed as a two-word
sentence — is not one speakers volunteer unless someone points at it first.

## What it is doing in a corpus of real artefacts

There is no artefact. That is the entry.

Two of this field's most repeated cases turn out to be stories rather than
objects, and both are repeated by people who would not accept a claim on that
evidence in their own subject. A course that teaches reconstruction from
evidence has to be able to say which of its own examples are not evidence, on
the same page as the examples that are.

## The thing to notice

Both apocryphal entries flatter the reader. One says machines are stupid; the
other says a large company was careless in a way you would not have been.
Neither survives a citation chain. Whether that is a coincidence is week 3's
argument.
