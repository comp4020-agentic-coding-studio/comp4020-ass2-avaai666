<span lang="zh-Hans">干货</span> is dried food: mushrooms, scallops, dates. On
an aisle sign it became:

<blockquote data-specimen>
  <p>Fuck Goods</p>
</blockquote>

## Why the corpus holds two of these

Because the pair is the evidence and either one alone is an anecdote.

The duck and the aisle sign share a key, a merger and a ranking. They do not
share a register, a venue, a customer, or a translator. When one mechanism
produces the same failure across two unrelated contexts, the mechanism is the
thing that is real — which is the reconstruction move the whole course is built
on, made once, cleanly, in week 2.

## The obligation this creates

Two specimens with the same mechanism attribution have to be defensible
separately. If the aisle sign turns out to have been made by hand as a joke, it
does not weaken the duck; it removes itself from the corpus. Neither of these is
`primary` and the pair does not become stronger by being a pair.
