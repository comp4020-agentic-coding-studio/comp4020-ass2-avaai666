## The brief

> Keep a record of one artefact a week for eleven weeks, to a standard that
> would let somebody who was not there check it.

The log is the spine of the course. Everything else is written out of it.

An entry is not a paragraph about a funny sign. It is a record with four parts:
what the artefact said, where and when it was recorded, how confident you are
that it exists as described, and what you think produced it. The fourth part is
where you are allowed to be wrong, and it is the only part where being wrong is
free — as long as it is labelled.

## What you submit

A single markdown file, or a directory of them, in the format used by the shared
corpus. Photographs where you have them, cropped as the policy requires.

Entries dated after their week are accepted and marked as what they are: a
record written from memory. Say so in the entry. A log that pretends eleven
Wednesdays went perfectly is less credible than one that does not.

## What we are not looking for

Volume. Eleven good records beat thirty, and a week where you found nothing and
wrote down what you looked for is a legitimate entry.
