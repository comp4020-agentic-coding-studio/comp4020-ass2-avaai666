## The brief

> Put the corpus on a wall for a stranger.

## The visitor

Someone from elsewhere in the school. They have not been to a lecture, they do
not know what a phrase table is, and they will give you about six minutes.

They are not the reason to simplify. They are the reason the arrangement has to
carry the argument, because they will read three cards, not fifteen, and which
three is decided by where you put them.

## Curation

Eight to fifteen specimens. The order is the argument: a wall that runs
chronologically is making a claim about progress, and a wall grouped by
mechanism is making a different one. Either is fine. Neither by accident.

## Wall text

Two hundred words at the entrance, and a card per specimen. The card carries the
provenance on its front. A verification level printed on the back of a card, or
left in a folder, presents a specimen as more certain than it is, and that is
the one failure this brief does not forgive.

## The exclusions

The exhibit documents what it left out and why. Week 9's bench produces that
list and it is part of the submission, not an appendix to it. A curated corpus
that does not say what it excluded has not been curated.
