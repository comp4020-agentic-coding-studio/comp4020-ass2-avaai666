## The brief

> Take one artefact from the corpus and argue backwards from it to the process
> that produced it. Be specific enough to be wrong.

Not a survey. Not three artefacts compared. One.

## What a strong reconstruction does

It commits. *A lookup table with these senses in this order* is a claim somebody
could disprove. *Machine translation is imperfect* is not a claim at all, and it
is what most first drafts say in their final paragraph.

It also says what would falsify it. If a photograph surfaced showing the sign
was hand-painted by the owner, what in your argument survives? Answering that in
the piece is worth more than another paragraph of support.

## Marking

**Provenance** is the record: does the artefact hold up, and is its verification
level the right one rather than the flattering one?

**Argument** is the reconstruction: is it specific, does it follow from the
evidence, and is the line between evidence and inference visible on every page?

**Scope** is judgement: one artefact taken all the way, rather than four taken a
short distance. A piece that widens in its last three paragraphs to say
something general about language and technology loses marks here, and almost
every draft tries it.

## What you submit

A written piece of 1500–2000 words, and the corpus entry it is built on if that
entry is yours.
