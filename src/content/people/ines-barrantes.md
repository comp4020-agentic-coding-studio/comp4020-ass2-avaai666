I interpreted in courtrooms for eleven years. The part that stayed with me was
not the hard sentences. It was the moment a barrister repeated back what I had
said, slightly differently, and nobody in the room could tell which version was
now on the record.

That is the whole course. A translation is a claim about another text, and most
of the time there is no way to check it from the inside. The errors are the
exception. They are the only place the process shows.

I mark for provenance before I mark for insight, which students find harsh in
week 3 and reasonable by week 9.
