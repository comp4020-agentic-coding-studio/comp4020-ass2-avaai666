## Provenance

Every specimen you submit carries a source, a date, and a verification level:
`primary`, `secondary` or `apocryphal`. A specimen with no provenance is not
marked down. It is not marked.

`apocryphal` is not a penalty. Several of this field's best-known examples are
apocryphal, and establishing that is the work rather than a failure of it. What
is penalised is presenting one as though it were sourced.

## Machine translation in your own submissions

You may use machine translation in your coursework, and most of you will.
Declare the system, the direction, and the date, in the same form you use for a
specimen. A system's output changes between versions, so an undeclared date
makes your evidence unreproducible by anyone including you. This is a
provenance rule, not a suspicion.

## Photographing signage

Most of what this course collects is in public. That does not make all of it
yours to publish.

- Photograph the sign, not the people under it. If someone is identifiable and
  incidental, crop them out before the specimen enters the corpus.
- Shop interiors, menus and staff areas are private property. Ask.
- Record the place at the granularity you are willing to publish. "A noodle
  restaurant, Dickson, March 2027" is a provenance. A street address is a
  person's workplace.

## Late work

Benches cannot be submitted late, because a bench is a room with people in it.
Written work can: ask before the due date and a short extension is routine. Ask
afterwards and it is an extenuating circumstances matter, which takes longer and
is worse for everybody.

## Academic integrity

The corpus is shared. Using another student's specimen is expected and
encouraged, on the same terms as any other source: cite it, and do not inherit
its verification level without checking it yourself.
